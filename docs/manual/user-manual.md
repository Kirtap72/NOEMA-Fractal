# NOEMA Fractal – user manual

This manual describes the program as it looks and works in this version. Buttons and menu items are written **like this**. You can always read it without a network: **Help ▸ User manual (offline)** (Shift+F1). Press **Ctrl+K** in the program to search all settings.

## 1. Quick start (5 minutes)

1. Start the program with **Start-NOEMA-Fractal.cmd**. It opens NOEMA with your own profile (your artworks are under **File ▸ My fractals**).
2. Look at the picture. Drag with the left mouse button to orbit around the fractal, and roll the mouse wheel to zoom in and out.
3. Choose **Start with a ready-made fractal** on the Start tab, or open your own scene with **File ▸ Open scene…**. Your personal library is initially empty.
4. If you want help from AI: open the **AI Studio** tab in the top bar (chapter 12).
5. Save when you are happy: **File ▸ Save scene as…** (Ctrl+Shift+S).

> Everything the AI does happens in a separate **draft**. Your scene only changes when you press **Use** yourself, and it can be undone.

## 2. The window and the menus

- **The artist view.** At the top there are eight tabs: **Start**, **Shape**, **Material**, **Light**, **Camera**, **View**, **Render**, **AI Studio** (Alt+1 to Alt+8). Each tab shows only what belongs to the task; **Advanced** (top right) shows all panels in one long list, exactly as described below, and **Simple view** goes back. They are the same settings all the time, so nothing you changed disappears. Under **Start** there are five quick changes (*More detail*, *Softer shapes*, *More dramatic light*, *Richer colours*, *Explore variations*) and presets for light, material and colour with pictures of your own fractal. Each quick change says what it changed, and can be undone in one step.
- **The left menu** has eight sections: *Camera & focus*, *Fractal & geometry*, *Material & textures*, *Light*, *Atmosphere*, *Preview & rendering*, *Animation* and *Library & export*. Click a section name to jump there. **Hide panel** (top left) gives the picture the whole window.
- **The categories fold and unfold.** Click the heading (▾ open, ▸ folded). The first time only *Fractal & geometry* is open so that you see a short overview. Under the search box there are **Open all categories**, **Collapse all categories**, **Open all parts** and **Close all parts**; each category has its own **Open all parts** / **Close all parts** buttons for its parts and sections. Folding only hides: no value is changed. What you left open or closed is remembered by the program until next time (on this computer, not in the scene).
- **Ctrl+K** opens the search box **Search settings…**: type for example *roughness* or *depth of field* and jump straight to the setting. If you find a setting in a folded category, the category, the part and the section around it open automatically.
- **The box above the picture** ("Explore / Create / Inspect / Final Preview") chooses how the picture is shown (chapter 9). F1–F4 switch between the four modes from any tab without changing the camera or the scene. The button **Hide settings ▴** folds it into a narrow strip; **Show preview settings ▾** opens it again. The choice is remembered until next time and never affects the scene or the camera.
- **AI Studio** (a tab of its own in the top bar) shows the AI panel in the side panel. You can switch tab without the conversation being interrupted. **View** collects preview, quality, Compare lights, focus mode, diagnostics and **Appearance** (dark and light themes, patterns and a background of your own for the outer edges of the program; visibility, darkening and panel density are controlled separately).
- The **File** menu: *Open scene…*, *Save scene as…*, *Recent scenes*, *My fractals*, *Material library…*, *Import library…*, *Export library…*, *Show library folder*, *Quit*. The **Help** menu: *About / graphics status*, *User manual (offline)*, *Export feedback (local)…*.
- **Undo** and **Redo** (top right and in the left menu) step backwards and forwards among the latest 20 changes. A coherent change (a drag on a slider, a typed value, a click on a button) is one step. Undo applies to formula values, material, colour and gradient, light, fog, environment, camera, objects, displacement, cut and render settings, quality, as well as AI drafts and material library choices. Camera movement with the mouse in the picture and a drag of the handles of a clip plane are one step each, and Undo also moves the camera back. Fly mode (WASD) is not included. When you open another scene the history is emptied.

## 3. Navigating and controlling the camera

- **Rotate**: drag with the left mouse button. **Move the target point**: right button or Shift+drag. **Zoom (dolly)**: the mouse wheel. **Fine control**: Shift or Ctrl + the mouse wheel.
- **Focus**: double-click a detail and it becomes the new centre for rotation and sharpness.
- The section **Camera & focus** has *Fly through the fractal*, lens buttons (**18, 24, 35, 50, 85, 100 mm**), **Reset camera** and four saved camera views (**Save camera view 1–4**, **Fetch camera view 1–4**).
- **Camera / composition** controls the picture format and the crop. If you change the format the frame changes, not the fractal itself.
- **Focus and depth of field (DOF)**: click **Focus here** and then the picture, or choose **Deep / Moderate / Shallow / Very shallow**. **DOF from navigation focus** takes the sharpness from what you last focused on. Depth of field makes the preview slower and is not verified together with Geometry Detail.

**Exercise:** open an artwork, press **Reset camera**, choose **85 mm**, double-click an interesting detail and save the view with **Save camera view 1**.

## 4. Formulas, parameters and colours

- **Fractal & geometry** shows the formula chain. Each step has a formula (for example *Mandelbulb*), a number of iterations and its own **Parameters**. Drag the sliders and the picture updates directly.
- **＋ Add formula** adds a step (at most eight). The arrows move steps up and down, **Remove step** removes one. **Change formula in step** changes the formula. **Favourite** marks a formula. In the formula picker a small picture shows what the formula gives when you choose it (its own default scene). A **transform** (a formula that only moves the point) is shown behind a fixed default formula, which is stated on the picture ("on Ikenaga bulb" and similar), so that you see what it does to a known shape. The pictures are finished files: the picker renders and compiles nothing, and a picture is fetched only when the row becomes visible.
- **Safety of the distance estimate:** mixtures where an IFS formula (folding and a fixed scale) is combined with another distance model, and interpolation of IFS formulas, are rejected with an explanation, because no safe distance estimate exists for them. Use a group execution or sequence/alternation instead.
- **Randomise a new chain** creates a random combination. **Import MB3D preset** reads a Mandelbulb 3D preset (maps parameters, no code); **Undo MB3D import** takes it back.
- The author and the source of a formula are shown in the program and follow saved scenes.
- **Colours**: the gradient in the section **Material & textures** (chapter 6) determines the colours. **Colour measure** chooses what the colour follows: iterations, orbit trap, slope, cavities or radius. **Repeats** and **Offset** move and repeat the gradient over the surface.

## 5. Material and PBR maps

- **The material** has *metal*, *roughness* and *reflectivity*. Low roughness gives a shiny surface, high gives matt. Metal 1 gives a metallic surface.
- **Texture set · PBR** puts picture maps on the surface: **colour (baseColor)**, **roughness**, **metal (metallic)**, **occlusion (ao)**, **emission** as well as normal and height. **Choose maps…** reads several maps at once and suggests the right channel from the file name (for example *diff*, *rough*, *nor_gl*).
- **Texture size** (scale) states how many times the maps repeat per unit. All maps in a material share the scale so that they stay aligned. **Texture size: reset to 1** resets it.
- **Material library…** (the File menu or in *Material & textures*) saves and fetches materials. Click a material to **try** it in the picture; **Apply material** does it for real, **Restore original** takes it back. **Undo apply** undoes. It works the same way for the whole fractal and for a chosen scene object (under *Scene objects*, choose **Object material: own**).
- **Why does it sometimes look as if nothing happened?** The colour of the surface comes from the colour gradient, and the colour of the material (and the colour map) is multiplied with it. A light material on an orange gradient therefore looks almost unchanged. The library says so and offers **Apply with the colour of the material itself**, which sets *Influence of the orbit colour* to 0 %. You can also lower it yourself under *Material*.
- **Surface Detail** (normal/bump map, procedural detail) changes the light normal of the surface. **Displacement · real geometry** moves the surface itself. Both are powerful but limit Geometry Detail (chapter 10).

## 6. Gradient and Material gradient

The gradient is the colour field in **Material & textures**.

1. Click in the field to add a **colour stop**; drag to move; **Remove colour stop** removes one. The colour is chosen with the colour box or a hex code.
2. **Material gradient:** choose a stop and click **Choose from the material library…**. There you find your saved materials, those you fetched from Poly Haven (the **Poly Haven** tab, where you can also fetch new ones) and search. Choose a material and click **Use for the chosen colour stop**: the stop becomes a material stop with the whole material, all picture maps and the source and license of Poly Haven, and it then works without a network. It can be undone with **Undo**. The short list under the button has the same materials (and the built-in ones) for quick choices.
   Note: a colour map is multiplied with **the colour of the stop itself**. If the stop is dark or coloured, the own colours of the material become weak; the button **Make the colour of the stop white** releases it.
   Result: the material is blended in. Then the metal, roughness, reflectivity, tone **and the picture maps** (colour, roughness, metal, occlusion) of the material are blended along the gradient – for example *stone that slowly becomes polished bronze*.
3. **Transition width** (how large a part of the stop is blended): 100 % blends over the whole interval, lower gives a tighter transition, 0 % a hard limit.
4. **Softness**: 0 % gives a straight transition, 100 % a soft S-curve. **Default** removes the softness.
5. **Texture scale** (shown when the material has picture maps): the scale for the material of that stop.
6. The row under the sliders says which maps are blended and how many textured materials are used (**at most 4 different** per gradient; the same material on several stops counts once).

**What is not blended:** normal/bump maps, emission, displacement, procedural detail and transparency. They are listed on the stop, they never disappear silently. The material transition is sharpest at about 3 % of the gradient. The gradient applies to the fractal, not to scene objects (spheres, boxes, planes). **Geometry Detail is turned off for scenes with a textured gradient** (the program says so).

**Exercise – stone to bronze:** fetch *Rock Boulder Dry* and *Metal Plate 02* from Poly Haven (chapter 7), choose the colour measure **radius**, set stop 1 to stone and stop 2 to the metal plate, reduce **Transition width** to 35 %.

## 7. Poly Haven – free materials

Poly Haven (polyhaven.com) has free PBR materials under the license **CC0**. NOEMA always shows "Powered by Poly Haven" and preserves the author and the link in the material.

1. **File ▸ Material library…** and choose the **Poly Haven** tab.
2. Search (for example *rock*, *moss*, *metal*), choose a category and a resolution (**1k** is recommended).
3. Click a material: you see a preview, which maps it **really** has (colour, roughness, metal, occlusion, normal) and which are not blended in gradients. Not all materials have all maps.
4. **Fetch … and put in My materials** fetches the files (a few MB) and puts the material in your library. Then click it under **My materials** to try it and **Apply material**.
5. **Offline:** fetched materials lie in a local cache and in your library. They work without a network. **Remove from the cache** frees space (the material in *My materials* remains).
6. The normal map is fetched but **turned off** (the orientation is not verified). Displacement is not imported.

The program only contacts the official addresses of Poly Haven, checks the downloads (size and checksum) and sends nothing about you or your artworks.

## 8. Light, shadows, atmosphere and Artist Looks

- **Light**: **+ Directional** (like the sun), **+ Point** (like a lamp) and **+ Spot** (spotlight) add lights (at most 8). Each light has colour, strength, direction/position and shadows (none, hard, soft). **Ambient light** gives an even base lighting.
- **Light rigs** place several lights starting from the camera (**Subdued, Normal, Strong, Very strong**, **Use rig**).
- **Looks (material, light and fog)**: a *Look* is a saved mood. **Save Look**, **Export Looks…**, **Import Looks…**; **Restore original** and **Undo apply** take back a Look you tried.
- **Atmosphere**: fog (colour, density, start) and sky/environment. Search *fog* or *environment* with Ctrl+K.
- **Depth of field** (chapter 3) belongs to the camera.

## 9. Display modes and final rendering

NOEMA has four display modes. The box above the picture (the View tab) has a button for each, and under the buttons there is a short explanation of the mode you chose. Click an active mode again to go back to Explore. F1–F4 choose the modes in this order from any tab.
- **Explore**: the fast ordinary viewport, for moving the camera and feeling the shape. The light is simplified and the picture is not final quality. Best when you look for a good view. The quality is chosen with *Viewport* next to the buttons.
- **Create**: build and experiment. Change formulas, parameters, material, colour, light, objects, glass, cut and displacement and see the result directly, without waiting. The lighting is simplified (see the section *Create* further down). Best when you create.
- **Inspect**: the same surface as in the final picture, and light, shadows and materials can be adjusted without anything being recomputed; the picture gets cleaner with every sample. Best for heavy fractals and when you fine-tune the look.
- **Final Preview**: as close to the final render as possible: the same geometry, reflections and refinement as the final picture (the hit limits are recalculated from the size of the real final picture), but with a size of its own (512, 768 or 1024 px) and a sample count of its own. Under the settings it says what differs from the final render (the size and samples of the final picture, Geometry Detail, denoising). Choose *Follow the setting of the scene* to get the same Geometry Detail as the final picture. The picture is drawn in three steps while the camera stands still: first a fast overview (512 px, 1 sample), then the chosen size and last a refined picture; the latest finished picture stays until a better one is ready. Best before a final render.
- While the camera moves, Final Preview shows the fast view labelled **Approximation**; as soon as the camera stands still the geometry of the final picture is drawn (**FINAL PREVIEW**). If you change light or material while a new shader is being built, the new light is first drawn as an approximation and is replaced by the exact picture when it is ready.
- **Render still picture** (in *Preview & rendering*) makes the final picture in the chosen size; **Test render (small)** makes a fast trial picture. **Cancel** stops a rendering in progress.
- **Render region:** the button in the top bar (visible in all tabs) lets you drag a rectangle in the picture and render only that area at the real final resolution and quality, with Geometry Detail if it is on in the scene. Esc cancels.
- The final settings (picture size, quality) are not changed by the preview.
- **Quality level** (at the top of *Still picture & quality*): choose **Draft**, **Good**, **Fine**, **Adaptive fine** or **Extra fine**, or set **Geometry**, **Anti-aliasing** and **Shadows** separately. Finer geometry gives the most visible difference; more samples only smooth edges. **Adaptive anti-aliasing** puts the samples only where there are edges and thin parts and saves time on pictures with a lot of even surface. Reflections, fog and material are not affected. New scenes start at **Fine** (4 samples).
- **Render** (at the top of the Render tab): **Render still picture**, **Progressive rendering** (a picture that gets better and better, growing in the viewport; **Stop** keeps the best finished picture, **Save best picture** saves it; a changed camera cancels) and the levels **Draft**, **Good**, **Fine**, **Extra fine** and **Maximum**. **Maximum** lowers the Float32 floor (the field *Precision floor*) and gives more fine structure in deep zooms; after a rendering it says if the floor limited the picture. **Whole picture: Geometry Detail only in the cut-out** puts Geometry Detail in a chosen cut-out and ordinary rendering in the rest.
- **Sculpture modes** (the Light tab, at the top): ready-made lightings for a sculpted picture: **Neutral detail review**, **Mandelbulb Classic** (one clear light obliquely from above left, dark nooks), **Sculpture**, **Harmonic sculpture**, **Dramatic sculpture** (low side light, deep light hierarchy), **High-contrast cinematic** (warm light against cool shadow), **Chiaroscuro** (a single strong light with real shadows over the whole shape, almost no fill; what the light does not reach becomes genuinely dark, lit edges keep the detail), **Golden hour** and **Moonlight**. All are optional, change only light and toning and can be undone. One click (one Undo step) sets light, shadows, occlusion, contact shadows and tone curve; geometry, camera, formulas and colours are not changed. The distances are calculated from the distance of the subject and the height of the picture, so they work the same in a deep zoom as in a wide view. Contact shadows are also available as an option per light (*Contact shadows (fast)*). **Wide occlusion**, **Contact shadow length** and **Curved environment lighting** fine-tune the position. **Calmer reflections** soften small, hard glitter where the surface is densely detailed (smooth surfaces keep sharp reflections; 0 = off, costs about one extra normal calculation per pixel) and **Occlusion of indirect light** lets in less ambient and sky light in deep nooks (0 = off, costs nothing). **Fit exposure** measures a small picture and sets the exposure so that the mid-tones of the surface become suitably bright; use it when a mode becomes too dark or bright for the subject.
- **Inspect** (formerly *Fast LookDev*; the View tab): for very heavy fractals such as reference scene, with **the same picture quality as Final Preview** (the same surface geometry and the same samples). The picture is built up of up to 16 samples and the surface is saved for every sample. If you change light, shadows (Off/Contact/Full, softness), main light against fill, material or the colour stops of the colour scale, **the already saved samples are reshaded** without the fractal being recomputed and without the shader being rebuilt, and you never see a new, noisy 1-sample picture: the old picture stays (dimmed and labelled OLD PICTURE if it takes a while) until **at least 4 reshaded samples** exist, and then the picture gets cleaner (8, 16 samples). When you stop adjusting, new samples are added. At 512 px it usually takes 0.1–0.3 s to the first 4 samples and 0.5–1 s to 16 samples; at larger pictures proportionally longer. If you change the camera, formulas or geometric parameters, the quality or the resolution, a new picture starts (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 samples) and the old saved surfaces are discarded. The first time on a new scene the surface and a special shader are built, which on reference scene takes about a minute. It is not the final picture: denoise and depth of field are missing, and the blue label says so. Reflections, glass, global illumination, own objects, displacement, formula fields, box cuts and procedural surface detail are drawn with the same shader as the final picture and give the same picture (measured: no pixel differs), even after you changed light, colour or material; if you move an object, change displacement, formula fields or clipping, a new picture starts. The button **Draw final quality** (next to the mode buttons) switches to Final Preview, which is unchanged. **Textures work:** maps for colour, roughness, metal, AO and emission, bump and normal maps and textured materials in the colour scale are applied after the hit, exactly as in the final picture. You can therefore adjust light, colours and materials while the textures are visible (measured: the picture is pixel for pixel equal to the final renderer, even after a light, colour or material change). If you switch between a material with textures and one without, a special shader is built once (a few seconds). No scenes are refused any more: own objects (also with textures), displacement, formula fields/height maps, procedural surface detail, box cuts and transparency with volume transport work in the same mode. The first time a scene with glass or several reflection bounces is shown, a special shader is built (a few seconds, longer on heavy scenes). If you work with reflections on (one bounce), the program builds the multi-bounce shader in the background, so if you raise the bounces afterwards it is already ready (measured in the real app: 1.8 s instead of a minute). If you have few **Reflection steps** (for example 40), a ray that runs out of steps counts as a hit where it ended up, so the reflections are filled by the surface instead of by sky or black; more steps give a more exact mirroring.
- **Light and shadow** (the Light tab, under Sculpture modes): the everyday controls for light and shadow, which work with *all* lightings, not only the ready-made modes. **Shadows: Off / Contact / Full** chooses how far the shadow of the main light reaches. *Contact* gives only short shadows in corners and nooks, so large shapes do not shade each other and the picture is evenly lit. *Full* gives shadows over the whole subject, so that large shapes shade each other and the picture gets clearly bright and dark parts (that is what the Chiaroscuro mode does). **Main light against fill** decides how large a share of all light comes from the main light; a low value gives flat light, a high value deep shadows (fill light and ambient light are scaled together). **Shadow depth** makes cavities and large shadow parts darker, **Ambient light** is the weak light that also reaches the shadows and **Shadow softness** makes the edge of a full shadow softer (0 = sharp). The shadows are cast by the main light, the strongest light; fill and rim lights are not affected. Every change is an Undo step and nothing but light is changed. In LookDev the fast overview is drawn first and a sharper picture follows; a full shadow takes longer to draw than a contact shadow.
- **Create** (formerly *Live LookDev 2*; the View tab): a new renderer built to create freely, alongside Inspect and the final rendering, which are unchanged. The formula exists in one place in a small shader, and everything else is data: light, shadows, material, colour scale, objects (sphere, box, plane, also rotated), reflection bounces, environment picture (HDRI), fog, **depth of field**, camera (perspective and orthographic) and formula parameters are redrawn directly without anything being built. **Cutting, object material, denoising and glass in Create:** clip planes and box cutting are drawn as in the final rendering (the cut surface shows the inside of the fractal). Spheres, boxes and planes get their own maps (colour, roughness, metal, occlusion, emission), bump and normal maps and procedural detail. The *Denoise* of the scene gives an edge-preserving filter over the samples already drawn (it filters less the more samples the picture has). Glass is drawn on spheres and boxes when *transport* is on: the rays are refracted into and out of the object with Fresnel and light absorption, and the picture follows enough bounces. Limitations: planes as glass are not drawn (as in the final rendering), light passes through glass without being attenuated, the fractal itself is not glass, and the deep double-precision mode draws neither clipping, glass nor displacement.

**Displacement on the fractal itself (the Shape tab, the part *Displacement on the fractal*):** add layers of noise (large organic shapes, ridges, cracks, erosion, pores, fine grain) that really change the shape of the fractal: the silhouette, the shadows and the reflections follow, and the final rendering draws exactly the same shape as Create. Each layer has *Depth*, *Size of the details*, *Detail levels*, *Sharpness* and a direction (*Out and in*, *Dig in*, *Build out*). Under *Where* you choose where on the fractal the layer acts: the whole fractal, *Orbit trap* or *Iterations* (the same measures as the colour gradient), height, distance from the centre or side/depth, with *From*, *To* and *Soft edge*. Several layers combine, so you can put large shapes on top and cracks or pores only on some parts. Everything is numbers: changing a layer builds no shader, so you see it directly in Create. Limitations: the 3D export (mesh) does not follow the displacement yet, and in the deep zoom mode in Create (double precision) the displacement is not drawn. Very steep noise (large depth against small details) makes the drawing slower; the panel says so. **Displacement and procedural surface detail:** the *displacement* of the material changes the geometry on own spheres and planes (as in the final rendering; the program does not displace the fractal itself with it, and neither does Create, so put a sphere or a plane in the scene and give it a material with a height map), and the *procedural detail* of the material (fine-noise, fbm, brushed, hammered, stone, ridged) is drawn on the surface of the fractal with the same rules as the final rendering. Both are numbers and pictures in buffers: if you change strength, scale, pattern or map you see it directly without anything being built. Objects (sphere, box, plane) do not yet get procedural detail or bump/normal maps, and displacement is not drawn on boxes or glass. **Textures and material:** the colour picture in the Colour tab, the texture maps of the materials (colour, roughness, metal, occlusion, emission, bump and normal) and the textured materials of the gradient follow the scene and are drawn with the own rules of the final rendering. Changing a picture or map takes a short while (the pictures are prepared, 0.1–1 s) but builds no shader. If you change formula only the small shader is built (under a second) while the picture stays. **The camera:** while you drag, coarser pictures (in a few fixed sizes) are drawn that follow the camera at an even pace, without waiting for a large picture that has already begun: the pictures are drawn in bands that can be interrupted after about 40 ms. The sharp picture comes when you stop moving, and a short cross-fade softens the switch. After a change the picture becomes sharper and calmer (16 samples, 32 with depth of field) while you stand still. Placing an object with *Add Sphere* finds the surface with the same small shader. **How close can you get:** the limit is set by the 32-bit numbers on the graphics card, not by the program: about three millionths of a unit when the surface is 4 units from the origin (four times closer than in the final rendering), and the surface becomes blocky near the limit. For formulas that have a double-precision version (the ABox family, RandCubesIFS, height maps and some helper formulas) a second, deep kernel is built in the background in about a second when the camera approaches the limit, and takes over when needed: then it is possible to get down to about a trillionth of a unit (a million times closer). The label says *deep precision (double)* while it is used. The deep picture is drawn at half width with fewer samples because double precision costs about ten times as much (0.3–2 s per picture on a heavy scene), and the kernel is rebuilt (about 1 s) when you change formula values while you are that close. Other formulas have no deep version: the label says *precision limit reached (32-bit numbers)* instead of the picture freezing. **Limitations:** the lighting is simplified and the picture is not the final picture: simpler occlusion, no cavity, no glass, no global illumination, the label says what is not yet drawn, no denoise (mirror-shiny fractals with several bounces are therefore grainier than in the final picture), and objects (sphere, box, plane) do not get textures yet. A final rendering switches to Final Preview.

- **The wheel flies forward** (Navigation / focus, the choice *Wheel*): the mouse wheel moves the camera and the focus point together forward at an even speed, so the camera does not brake against the surface or the focus point but goes straight through it (and backwards the other way). The speed is the distance to the focus point times a small share per wheel step: double-click the surface to get a suitable speed, **Alt + wheel** approaches/moves the focus and thereby changes the speed, Shift = finer, Ctrl = much finer. The choice *Approaches the focus* gives the old behaviour (each step is a share of the remaining distance, so the camera brakes and never arrives). The separate button *Fly through the fractal* (WASD) remains.
- **Fine control and exact values** (at the top of the settings panel, the choice *Fine control*): all arrows and sliders for formula values, objects and material, and also the scene object sliders, can be made 10, 100 or 1000 times finer. The arrows at the numbers (small, toned until you point at the field) and the arrow keys change the value by about one hundredth of its size (a power of ten), not by 1; **Shift** makes the step 10 times finer, **Alt** 100 times (both 1000), PageUp/PageDown 10 times coarser, and the mouse wheel in a selected field changes the value in the same way. A slider that is dragged with Shift or Alt (or with Fine control chosen) moves relatively: the same mouse movement gives only a tenth, hundredth or thousandth of the change.
- **After a final rendering** the program switches back to the LookDev mode you were working in (Create or Inspect) about 1.5 s after the rendering is finished. During the rendering it stands in Still LookDev, as before. If you choose a mode yourself in the meantime it stays.
- **The first time there is a wait, then not.** On a heavy fractal the driver of the graphics card builds a special shader per scene, and it takes 40–60 seconds the first time (reference scene measured on RTX 2080). After that it is saved and the next start takes a few seconds. If you switch between a lighting with and without sculpture/contact shadows it goes quickly back to the variant you just left. The View tab does not draw the previews of the start presets while LookDev is turned on.
- **Detail view 1:1** (in the Render tab, under the quality levels): inspect any area of the finished picture at real resolution without drawing the whole picture. **Pick area (drag in the picture)** or **Centre** chooses the area, the arrows move it and *Size of the area* changes it. The area is drawn step by step up to **Extra fine**, **Maximum** or **Ultra** (*Final level*) and the pixels are exactly those that the whole rendering gives. If you change light or material the area is redrawn quickly (can be turned off with *Redraw when light or material changes*); if you change the camera or the picture size the area is closed. **Geometry Detail in the area** draws the same area with Geometry Detail when the formulas support it, and **Show** switches between Float32 and Geometry Detail. The rows under the picture show time, a rough estimate for the whole picture and how many 32-bit values per pixel Float32 has in the area; **Save the area** saves the area as PNG.
- **Adapt the precision to the size of the picture** and **High detail (Float32)** control how accurately the surface is calculated; Float32 is the safe choice for all formulas.

## 10. Geometry Detail and its limits

Geometry Detail gives extra fine surface geometry with double precision. It only works for certain formula chains. The program says **why** when it does not work (for example "scene objects are part of it", "transport/GI", "depth of field", "normal map", "textured gradient") and what you can do instead: **High detail (Float32) – not Geometry Detail** works for all formulas. The previews always run with Geometry Detail off.

## 11. Saving, library, import and export

- **Save scene as…** writes a `.scene.json` with everything: formulas, colours, material (embedded pictures), light, camera and source references. Scenes with Poly Haven maps can be several MB.
- **File ▸ My fractals** is the library of your artworks and thumbnails. **Save picture preset** puts the current view there. **Save as preset** / **Save to file** saves the formula chain.
- **Import library… / Export library…** moves whole libraries as a backup. **Show library folder** opens the folder.
- **Library & export**: **glTF (.glb)** and **JSON** for export, **Create variations**, **Save scene**.
- Never open a scene file you do not trust without understanding that it only contains data (no programs), but large pictures take memory.

## 12. Creating with AI

Open the **AI Studio** tab. The panel **Conversation with AI** has:
- **Provider** and **Model** (recommended models have ★ and show whether they can do tools and pictures). Change between attempts when you want.
- **Four modes**: **Create for me** (the AI tries several ideas itself, at most about ten pictures), **Explore shape** (the AI tries different formula chains in 2–3 drafts and compares them, see below), **Create together** (a few steps at a time and a question to you), **Teach me** (the AI explains and changes nothing; **Show as example** gives a small example in a draft).
- **Budget**: limits for the number of tool calls, pictures, time and tokens, so that the AI does not run away.
- **Choose reference picture…**: a picture as inspiration for mood, colour, material and composition. It is sent to the AI service only when you press **Send** (a downscaled copy, never saved). The AI does not rebuild the exact 3D shape of the picture; NOEMA is a fractal engine.
- **Let the AI search and fetch Poly Haven materials**: off at every start. Turn it on if you want the AI to be allowed to fetch materials (at most 20 searches and 6 downloads per session).

**How you work:**
1. Write what you want, for example *"Make the picture warmer and more dramatic, with stone and bronze"*, and press **Send**.
2. You see the steps **Understands → Edits the draft → Draws a preview → Reviews the result**, a large preview, a short explanation and a list **Actual changes** (for example *Light 1: intensity: 0.88 → 2.5*).
3. Reply naturally: *"less metal"*, *"keep the shape but change the light"*, *"try another Poly Haven stone"*. The AI continues in **the same draft**.
4. Buttons: **Continue**, **Change direction**, **Create variation** (a new draft from the current one), **Use**, **Discard**.
5. **Use** requires two clicks (the first asks). Then your scene is replaced by the draft; **Undo scene change** goes back. Drafts you do not use never change your artworks.
6. Drafts, conversation and settings remain if you close the program. The panel **AI bridge** (further down in the AI panel) lists the drafts with **Use**, **Reject**, **Discard** and shows a message if drafts were restored after a restart. Previews are not saved (ask for a new one).
7. If you want to start from a saved artwork: ask the AI to list your artworks and start from one. It is a **copy**; the original and its thumbnail are never changed.

### Explore shape – the AI tries different fractal shapes

In the mode **Explore shape** the AI changes the shape itself, not only colours, light and camera. It can change formula, insert, move and remove steps in the formula chain, change the number of rounds and the mode of the chain, and adjust the parameters in each step. Everything happens in separate drafts; your scene never changes until you press **Use** yourself.

- The AI first looks in the **knowledge atlas** (a local, versioned collection). There each entry is labelled as *fact* (checked in the engine), *observation* or *hypothesis* (an untested idea) and shows how certain it is. The atlas is guidance, never a barrier: the AI may try other combinations.
- The **knowledge base** contains built-in formula metadata and general engine guidance. Private collections, preset recipes and recorded research experiments are not included. Your own local experiment memory starts empty.
- It makes 2–3 drafts with clearly different chains, and each draft gets a line about **which geometric idea** it tests. The comparison also gives a colour-independent measure of how different the shapes are (silhouette and fine structure), so a repainted copy does not count as a new shape.
- What it tried is saved in a small local **experiment memory** (chain, idea, what it saw, verdict; no pictures and no conversation). It can be read by any model later.
- The box **Let the AI see statistics about my own artworks** is off at every start. If it is turned on the AI only learns how often formulas and pairs have been used in your saved artworks, never pictures, titles or parameters, and nothing leaves the computer by itself.
- If the budget runs out the drafts remain and can be edited; write *continue* for a new budget.

A suggestion to write: *"Explore shape: take my current fractal as the starting point and try three really different formula chains, for example a fold before the bulb, a lattice formula and a rotated mixture. Tell me which geometric idea each draft tests and show me the comparison."*

### Connect Ollama Cloud

1. Create an API key at ollama.com (your account, "API keys").
2. In the AI panel: choose the provider **Ollama Cloud** and click **Connect Ollama Cloud**. Paste the key and press **Save key**.
3. The key is **encrypted by Windows** for your account, is never shown again and is not saved in any project files or logs. **Test connection** sends a single very small request. **Remove saved key** deletes it.
4. Alternative: set the environment variable `OLLAMA_API_KEY` (and restart NOEMA). A saved key takes precedence over the environment variable.
5. Choose a model in the list. Recommended first choices: **deepseek-v4.1-flash**, otherwise **glm-5.3-flash** or **minimax-m3.1-flash**. All three are stated by Ollama Cloud as tool- and picture-capable, but how good a model is at steering NOEMA you only know when you have tried.
6. OpenAI exists as an alternative (the environment variable `OPENAI_API_KEY` or **Connect OpenAI**).

The app works exactly as usual without AI and without a key.

## 13. Troubleshooting

- **The picture is black or the program says the graphics card is missing**: NOEMA needs WebGPU. Open **Help ▸ About / graphics status**. Update the graphics driver and close other programs that use a lot of graphics memory.
- **The preview is slow**: choose **Explore** or **Fast 512 px** and 1 sample; turn off depth of field; the first picture after a change takes longer (the shader is compiled).
- **Poly Haven says offline**: the saved catalogue is shown; already fetched materials work. Check the network and the firewall.
- **"Key missing" / the AI does not answer**: connect the key (chapter 12) and press **Test connection**. 401/403 means a wrong or inactive key, 429 out of credit or too many calls, 404 that the model does not exist.
- **The AI changed nothing in my scene**: that is intended; look at the draft and press **Use**.
- **Geometry Detail cannot be turned on**: read the explanation in the program (chapter 10).
- **The scene is large**: pictures from materials are embedded. Use a lower resolution (1k) or fewer materials.
- **Help to report a problem:** **Help ▸ Export feedback (local)…** creates a file that you see yourself before it is saved. Nothing is sent automatically.
